# Screen two — the four instruments, as one set

## What is wrong with the ones on the board now

They were never drawn as a set of four. They are four survivors of a set of
nine, and side by side at this size the mismatches are the first thing you see:

1. **The handpiece is the odd one out twice over.** It is bare chrome where the
   other three are matte, and it is the only one with no face. It reads as a
   real dental drill sitting in a row of toys, which is the exact impression
   this screen exists to avoid.
2. **No two are the same size.** The mirror fills its panel, the checker is a
   thin sliver across the middle of its own, the gel bottle sits small and
   squat. Nothing tells a child these are four of a kind.
3. **They face different ways** — one lies flat, one stands, one tips.
4. **They are the old palette.** The clinic room and its instruments are now
   muted cream, pale blue and mint; these are the brighter, glossier earlier
   pass.

## Generate all four in one image

One image, not four. Four separate renders drift apart no matter how carefully
each prompt is written; four drawn together in one frame come out as a set,
which is the entire problem here. I cut them apart afterwards.

**Attach `app/public/art/clinic-layer-suction.webp` and
`clinic-layer-syringe.webp`** — the two instruments already in the clinic room.
They are the style target: matte, muted, softly lit. Everything below is
described against them.

```
Four toy dental instruments in a soft 3D clay-render style, arranged in a single horizontal row on a plain flat white background, evenly spaced with clear empty space between them so that no two touch or overlap.

Match the attached references exactly for finish, palette and lighting: matte clay surfaces, muted cream, pale blue, soft mint and a little coral, one soft light from the upper left with gentle falloff, and a soft contact shadow under each. No chrome, no polished metal, no glossy highlights.

From left to right:
1. A little dental mirror — a round mirror head on a slim cream handle with a pale blue grip.
2. A tooth checker — a slim probe with a gently curved tip, cream body, soft mint grip.
3. A sleepy gel bottle — a small rounded pump bottle in soft lilac and cream, with a rounded cap.
4. A polishing handpiece — a cream angled body with a soft round rubber cup on the end, in pale blue.

All four are the same height in frame, all standing at the same angle, all seen three-quarter from slightly above. Each has the same simple friendly face — two small dots and a tiny smile — in the same place on its body, at the same size.

Chunky, rounded and friendly. Nothing sharp, nothing that looks like a drill, nothing that reads as adult medical equipment.

No text, no letters, no numbers, no labels, no watermarks.
```

## The one that matters most

**Number four must not look like a drill.** Cream body, soft rubber cup, no
chrome. A child who is about to have one used on them is being shown it here on
purpose, and the whole point is that it looks like a little shower — which is
exactly what its line already says it is.

## The check before you send it

Cover the picture and uncover it a strip at a time. If any one of the four
jumps out as bigger, shinier or angled differently from its neighbours, that is
the one to fix. They have to read as four of a kind, because the board puts
them in a two-by-two grid where a child sees all four at once.

Send it over and I cut the four apart, drop them into the tray and rebuild the
board — one script, no code changes.
